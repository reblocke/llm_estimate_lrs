# Analysis specification

**Specification ID:** `analysis-spec-v1`
**Status:** draft pending independent statistical review
**Governs:** frozen accepted-paper analysis at release `v1.0.0`

## Authority and change control

This document describes the analysis that already exists. It does not revise
the accepted paper, notebooks, data, prompts, methods, or results. For
executable details, the frozen inputs, configuration, accepted notebooks, and
`scripts/compute_reference_results.py` are authoritative. Differences between
those artifacts and manuscript wording are disclosed rather than silently
harmonized.

Any change to the population, transformations, estimands, formulas, model
scope, random-number contract, tolerances, or accepted outputs requires
scientific approval and version reclassification. The independent statistical
review required to approve this specification remains
`PENDING_HUMAN_REVIEW`.

## Scientific question

The accepted study asks how closely diagnostic likelihood ratios generated by
three language-model configurations agree with literature-reported likelihood
ratios curated from TheNNT. It evaluates group-level bias, item-level
dispersion, calibration, qualitative agreement, and selected sensitivity
analyses. It does not establish clinical validity for model-generated
likelihood ratios, validate unstudied findings, or test combined multivariable
diagnostic updating.

## Unit of analysis and population

The unit of analysis is one accepted condition–finding–reported-LR row from the
`Master` sheet of `NNT_LRs_08-26-2025.xlsx`. The frozen population contains 700
rows across 30 full condition labels. All 700 rows are retained in workbook
order and are assigned stable IDs `lr_0001` through `lr_0700`.

Each row has one literature-reported LR and one output from each accepted
manuscript model:

- GPT-4o (`gpt-4o-2024-11-20`);
- o3 (`o3-2025-04-16`); and
- GPT-5 (`gpt-5`).

The GPT-4.1 column preserved in the historical workbook is auxiliary and is not
part of the accepted three-model analysis.

## Source-data construction

Literature-reported LRs were collected from TheNNT on 1 April 2025, initially
extracted automatically, and then subjected to duplicate independent manual
validation and reconciliation. The exact source page, table, and row-level
retrieval timestamp were not retained for every record.

`NNT_LRs_08-26-2025.xlsx` is the numerical source for the accepted analysis.
`nnt_lrs_with_estimated.xlsx` supplies recoverable per-condition worksheet
context and full condition labels. The canonical CSV is an additive,
one-to-one export of the accepted workbook; it does not replace either
workbook.

The deterministic crosswalk key combines finding, reported LR, all three
accepted model outputs, and the occurrence index among repeated values. A
non-unique or missing match is an error. Fuzzy matching is not permitted.

## Inclusion, exclusion, repeated rows, and missingness

All accepted `Master`-sheet rows are included. Apparently repeated
condition–finding combinations remain separate when the source workbook
contains them in distinct rows or contexts. No row is deduplicated based on
content similarity.

Raw strings remain in `*_raw` fields. Curated analysis or display values occupy
separate fields. A curated difference requires a recoverable curation record;
otherwise the accepted workbook value is preserved and the unavailable
rationale is recorded as a provenance gap.

Blank provenance fields mean unavailable. They do not mean that the event,
source, response, retry, or review did not occur. Missing provenance is never
inferred.

## Reported-LR transformation

All analyzed LRs must be finite and strictly positive. Analyses use the natural
logarithm. When TheNNT supplied a point estimate, that point estimate was
retained. When only a range was available, the accepted workflow used the
geometric mean of the endpoints. The repository does not reconstruct which
individual values were transformed when the historical row-level rationale is
unavailable.

## Model configurations and prompts

The accepted query date is 25 August 2025. Exact model identifiers, settings,
few-shot selection, and historical prompt text are preserved in
`config/manuscript_models_v1.json` and `prompts/main_estimator_v1.json`.
Historical wording and spelling are immutable research artifacts.

The accepted GPT-5 identifier is an alias. A later live query may resolve to
different provider behavior and would be a new replication, not reproduction.
No model, prompt, parameter, or missing response metadata may be substituted.

## Primary estimand and comparison direction

The primary outcome is agreement between `LR_reported` and `LR_model` on the
natural-log scale.

The Bland–Altman difference is:

```text
log(LR_reported) - log(LR_model)
```

The mean and limits are exponentiated and therefore reported as
`LR_reported / LR_model` ratios. Unity indicates no mean multiplicative bias.
The separate log-error summaries use the opposite direction,
`log(LR_model) - log(LR_reported)`, so positive log error means the model value
is larger.

## Agreement analysis

For each model, the accepted analysis calculates the mean and sample standard
deviation of the Bland–Altman log ratio. The 95% multiplicative limits are:

```text
exp(mean_log_ratio ± 1.96 * sample_sd_log_ratio)
```

Error summaries include mean, median, mean absolute, and median absolute log
error; log-scale RMSE; and exponentiated typical and RMSE error factors.
Coverage intervals at 50%, 75%, 90%, 95%, and 99% use normal quantiles around
the mean log ratio. Confidence intervals around each limit use a 95% Student-t
multiplier and the accepted limit standard-error formula.

## Pairwise comparisons

Pairwise model comparisons use paired vectors of
`log(LR_reported) - log(LR_model)` in notebook presentation order GPT-4o, o3,
GPT-5. The accepted outputs include:

- a paired t test and 95% confidence interval for mean difference in bias;
- the Pitman–Morgan test based on the correlation between paired sums and
  differences;
- the Bradley–Blackwood joint test of equality of means and variances; and
- descriptive widths of each model's 95% limits of agreement.

All tests are two-sided. Rows must be paired and finite.

## Evidence-direction analysis

Negative evidence is defined as `LR_reported < 1`; positive evidence is
`LR_reported > 1`. Rows equal to 1 are excluded from this comparison. For each
model, the accepted analysis compares mean bias using a two-sided Welch
independent-samples t test and dispersion using a median-centered Levene
(Brown–Forsythe) test.

The accepted manuscript sentence reverses the o3 negative/positive stratum
labels relative to the executable output while retaining the same rounded pair
and inference. This is disclosed as `KI-001`; neither artifact is relabeled.

## Calibration analysis

Forward calibration regresses `log(LR_model)` on `log(LR_reported)` using
ordinary least squares with an intercept. Outputs include intercept, slope,
R-squared, observed-to-expected ratio, calibration slope, and calibration in
the large.

The point estimates use the closed-form OLS implementation in
`scripts/compute_reference_results.py`. Percentile confidence intervals use
2,000 row-bootstrap samples.

## Inverse reliability analysis

Inverse reliability calibration regresses `log(LR_reported)` on
`log(LR_model)` with an intercept. This answers what literature-reported LR is
expected for a model output of a given magnitude. It is not interchangeable
with forward calibration. Intercept and slope confidence intervals use 2,000
row-bootstrap samples.

## Qualitative agreement

Qualitative bands and boundary inclusivity are defined by
`config/analysis_categories_v1.json`. That configuration is authoritative over
rounded prose descriptions. Agreement uses quadratically weighted Cohen's
kappa over the ordered seven-band scale. The normal-approximation 95%
confidence interval is clipped to `[-1, 1]`. Exact agreement and agreement
within one adjacent tier are also recorded.

Feature classifications are overlapping memberships rather than mutually
exclusive categories. Their counts therefore need not sum to 700.

## Reliability zones

The central reliability zone is
`0.2 <= LR_reported <= 5.0`, including both boundaries. The extreme zone is
`LR_reported < 0.2 or LR_reported > 5.0`. The accepted analysis computes
forward OLS calibration, Pearson and Spearman association, log-error,
Bland–Altman, and log-scale concordance summaries within each zone. These are
sensitivity and descriptive analyses, not a clinical operating threshold.

## Laboratory-discrepancy analysis

The accepted descriptive analysis selects the ten largest GPT-5 absolute log
errors among findings classified as test results and matching the frozen
lab-like text pattern. It counts explicit thresholds/comparators, units, and
extreme reported LRs. It does not assign causal failure categories or replace
primary-source abstraction.

## Threshold sensitivity analysis

The threshold perturbation analysis is an exploratory, single-run GPT-5
sensitivity analysis using five case families and three variants per family.
Its cases, raw results, reviewer table, summaries, prompt, and interpretation
are frozen historical artifacts. Offline reproduction validates or copies
those artifacts; it never reissues the model requests.

The analysis does not distinguish de novo inference from memorization or
distributional familiarity and does not establish monotonic clinical
calibration.

## Randomness contract

Forward calibration and inverse reliability each initialize a separate
`numpy.random.default_rng(20260407)` generator and use 2,000 bootstrap
replicates. Each generator is then consumed sequentially in the executable
`MODEL_SPECS` order:

1. GPT-4o;
2. GPT-5;
3. o3.

This differs from the common manuscript presentation order. Reordering models,
sharing one generator between forward and inverse calibration, changing the
seed or generator, or assigning independent per-model streams changes the
frozen confidence intervals and is not permitted for v1 reproduction.

## Numerical tolerances

Machine comparisons of unrounded reference results use absolute tolerance
`1e-3` unless a more specific output contract applies. Displayed rounded
agreement values use tolerance `0.01`. Hash-governed historical artifacts
require exact SHA-256 equality. Tolerances are comparison rules, not permission
to update expected values.

## Diagnostics and assumptions

The accepted analyses assume positive finite LRs and paired observations. OLS,
t-test, correlation, limits-of-agreement, and bootstrap interpretations depend
on their usual distributional and independence assumptions. Repeated source
rows and multiple outputs per condition may create dependence not modeled by
these analyses. The reference comparator also omits much primary-study
context, uncertainty, patient spectrum, assay detail, and threshold detail.

These limitations constrain interpretation but do not authorize a post hoc
change to the accepted formulas.

## Prespecified, exploratory, and descriptive analyses

- Primary: log-scale Bland–Altman agreement and multiplicative limits.
- Secondary: forward calibration and qualitative agreement.
- Sensitivity: evidence direction, inverse reliability, reliability zones,
  coverage intervals, and pairwise bias/dispersion comparisons.
- Exploratory/descriptive: threshold perturbation and the laboratory
  discrepancy flag counts.

This classification records the accepted package's current evidence. An
independent statistical reviewer must confirm whether the labels fully match
the historical analysis plan.

## Known deviations and issues

The evidence-direction wording discrepancy is recorded as `KI-001`. Missing
source, response, retry, SDK, and curation provenance is recorded in
`data/provenance/provenance_gaps_v1.csv` and `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`. The accepted
notebooks retain duplicated analysis logic until a separately reviewed
successor implementation is created.

## Interpretation and safety limitations

The study measures agreement with curated reported values under minimal-context
prompting. It does not prove accuracy for unpublished or context-shifted
findings, support pointwise substitution for empirical LRs, validate clinical
decision support, quantify within-model stochasticity, or validate joint
multi-finding updating. Any live rerun is a new experiment subject to model
drift and separate governance.
